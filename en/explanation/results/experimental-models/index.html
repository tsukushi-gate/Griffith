Models from the experimental branches

Separately from the models that live in the package itself (= the first half of the Model Zoo), there are many configurations that only ever ran on experimental branches. This page is where they live. Unlike the mainline pages, the lead role here belongs to the negative results and the reason behind them. Nothing stops at “it did not help”: the purpose of this page is to record why it did not help, every single time.

The collection covers 11 families, 81 configurations (24 series if counted by experiment series ID). All of them were run from scripts under experiments/, and the metrics are logged in wandb.

How to read every family on this page

  • Most results are single-seed. Three seeds were taken only for LUT6-1 (the 1-layer condition), HYBRID-4b, RES-LN-2 (rezero-n2) and VAE-1 (logic-n6 / float-bern-match). No ranking within 0.3pt in the 0.980–0.984 band can be claimed.
  • Equal gate budget ≠ matched parameter count. N=2 softmax is 16 parameters per gate, N=6 is 64, and AP’s independent is 4. Always check this when comparing across tables.
  • Provisional values with unequal epochs are mixed in: the back-end scale runs at 16M (10ep) and 61M (5ep, cut short), and Logic-DenseNet’s CIFAR dense/plain (61ep, cut short).
  • All FPGA numbers are theoretical values (200MHz, one clock per layer, fully pipelined; an upper bound before place-and-route). Measured results from Vivado synthesis have not been performed.
  • The hybrid families are not on the same footing as the literature. They include a floating-point NN front-end (roughly 200k–1.65M parameters), and only what sits behind the binary interface can be put into hardware. A comparison that lines up gate counts alone against the literature — where the entire network is logic gates (difflogic / DWN / LogicTreeNet / LILogicNet) — does not hold. What can be said under matched conditions goes as far as “the back-end’s contribution given the same front-end and the same interface” and “the relative position against pure DLGN”.

1. Pure DLGN / input bypass — clever fixed wiring never won, not once

Section titled “1. Pure DLGN / input bypass — clever fixed wiring never won, not once”

What was tried: on the hypothesis that the weakness of pure DLGN (400-bit input → LutLayer×L → GroupSum, with only gate type being learned) lies in the random sparse connectivity of the input layer, wiring tricks that redistribute the raw input to every layer were tried across 5 rounds (R1–R5), about 30 runs. Conditions fixed at mnist20x20 (400 bits), n_inputs=2, τ=10, bs=128, seed 0, fixed wiring.

Why it did not work: input bypass is a prescription for “the information is not arriving”, but the actual bottleneck was the input layer itself, not the wiring structure. Mixing raw input into a layer with a fan-in of only 2 costs exactly that many slots for receiving the previous layer’s features. The fixed logic residuals OR / XNOR, far from creating an identity path, collapse the layer via OR(h,h)=h / XNOR(h,h)=1 (and pairing them with residual initialization accelerates the degeneration). The fact that mnist784 got worse settled it, and the motivation moved to the hybrid family: “put a densely connected NN in front and rebuild the input layer”.

2. Hybrids — front-end NN/CNN + binary interface + back-end DLGN

Section titled “2. Hybrids — front-end NN/CNN + binary interface + back-end DLGN”

What was tried: raise a bit interface of front-end NN (Linear+ReLU or Conv) → Sigmoid → Binarize(0.5, STE), and make everything behind it pure logic gates (a stack of LutLayer → GroupSum). Everything behind the interface drops straight into HDL. Six stages: front-end MLP (HYBRID-1), front-end CNN (HYBRID-2), back-end shape search (HYBRID-3), settling wiring and LUT6 (HYBRID-4/4b), and sweeping the GPU/FPGA boundary (HYBRID-5/6).

Structure (the actual sizes of the series best, h2-cnn-dlgn-expand = CIFAR-10 hard 0.7864)

How to read it: only what is to the right of the interface (green) is 1,120 two-input LUTs that lower to Verilog. The left (orange) is an ordinary CNN and needs a GPU. Against the all-NN control (h2-cnn-mlp, 0.7425), the claim of this series is +4.39pt from swapping only the back end from MLP to DLGN while keeping the front end identical — and it is also why gate counts here cannot be laid side by side with papers whose networks are logic gates end to end.

What it showed: the sparse-connectivity bottleneck is gone — +7.20pt over the matched-budget pure DLGN at 0.8994, and 0.9703 is reached with 560 gates (1/7 the size). On CIFAR, a separate learning rate for the front-end (--front-lr 1e-3) is mandatory.

What it showed: CNN+DLGN beat CNN+MLP by +4.39pt. The winner’s back-end is nothing but 1,120 two-input LUTs, and its front-end CNN is also 40% smaller than the control’s. The FPGA back-end’s latency is 20ns (5 stages @200MHz).

What it showed: there is a 32pt spread within the axis, so shape dominates (expanding ≫ constant > contracting). Under a constant shape, width and accuracy are inversely correlated: 500 gates at 0.9618 beat 2,000 gates at 0.9178. l6-expand is exactly equal, ±0.00pt, to n=2, so in this setting LUT6’s expressiveness is not the bottleneck. LUT6’s value lies on the other side: “the same accuracy with fewer gates” (560 vs. 2,000 = a 3.6× reduction).

Factor decomposition: cross-layer shortcuts +0.93pt > even coverage +0.49pt (which only helps LUT6) > interface 64→120 bits +0.36pt. Interface 120→240 bits is −0.29pt (saturated), and DWN-style learned wiring is −0.36pt.

Why it collapsed: BinaryBrain-style re-binarization between layers dies without a skip path. Combined with balanced wiring (previous layer only) it falls to hard 0.089–0.114, whereas with bypass it trains normally at 0.9721. Pushing gradients through many stages of STE with no detour makes them vanish.

HYBRID-4b (settled with 3 seeds + CIFAR): for MNIST legacy bypass, n=2 0.97430 vs. LUT6 0.97450 — the ranges overlap completely, so there is no difference. With balanced+bypass, n=2 0.97487 > LUT6 0.97300. On CIFAR-10, n=2 0.7864 > LUT6 (balanced+bypass) 0.78230 > LUT6 (legacy) 0.77555. In this hybrid configuration, “LUT6 > LUT2” does not hold (LUT6 is always at best equal, for 4× the parameters). The sign of balanced+bypass flips with the dataset (MNIST −0.150pt / CIFAR +0.675pt).

What it showed: the sweet spots are “Conv1 for speed, Conv4 for accuracy”. The intermediate Conv2 gives 15× worse speed return per point of accuracy and is not worth buying. “One conv + one Linear + everything else DLGN” works if the goal is speed (3.40×) and fails if the goal is accuracy (−5.0pt vs. all-NN / −8.7pt vs. Conv4). The information lost by making the boundary shallower is recovered neither by back-end depth (+0.52pt) nor by interface width (+0.32pt). train_acc is 0.983–0.999 across every run, so the accuracy difference is generalization, not a capacity shortfall. The bottleneck is always the GPU side (against 5.1µs of FPGA and 9.1µs of PCIe, the GPU part is 1,805µs at its fastest).

HYBRID-6 demonstrated the theoretical 3.40× as an actual 3.23–3.41× using a double-buffered event-driven schedule, and latency improved 11.35× at bs=1 (0.939ms → 0.083ms). Even swinging T_fpga by a factor of 100, the bottleneck stays in the GPU front-end at all 15 configurations × 4 points = 60 points: making the FPGA faster does not make the system faster. The only two effective levers are “shrink the GPU front-end” and “amortize the fixed PCIe term (6–13µs) over large batches”. On an LLM proxy (d384 / L6 / vocab50k), going from k=1 to k=64 concurrent sequences moves the LM head’s share from 20% to 49%, and the FPGA offload gain on prefill goes from 1.25x to 1.95x.

3. Back-end scale (HS-1) — inverse scaling

Section titled “3. Back-end scale (HS-1) — inverse scaling”

What was tried: keeping HYBRID-2’s front-end CNN and interface fixed, sweep only the back-end DLGN’s gate count over four orders of magnitude, from 1,120 to 61,000,000. The point was to reach the same budget band as LogicTreeNet-B (16M) and -G (61M) in the literature and check directly whether “more gates” means “better”. CIFAR-10, seed 0.

Back-end gatesDepthbest hardEpochs
1,120 (control = the HYBRID-2 configuration)30.780620
100,0002 / 4 / 80.7793 / 0.7808 / 0.776220
1,000,0004 / 80.7724 / 0.767920
8,000,00080.762720
16,000,000 (= the LogicTreeNet-B budget)80.730510
61,000,000 (= the LogicTreeNet-G budget)80.60085 (cut short)

Why it did not scale: at an equal 20 epochs the numbers get monotonically worse — 0.7806 → 0.7808 → 0.7724 → 0.7627 — which is inverse scaling. On the footing set by the front-end CNN, the back-end saturates at roughly 1,000 gates. The effect of depth is zero-to-negative across every budget band (d4 ≧ d8 everywhere). That said, 61M at 0.6008 is not invalid capacity but insufficient training (at the point it was cut short it was still gaining +5–7pt per epoch: 0.293→0.396→0.479→0.548→0.601). The comparison target, LogicTreeNet-G at 86.29%, is the product of 700–1000 epochs, which shows that the real cost of a huge gate network is not gates but training. On the engineering side, 61M gates (980M parameters) peaks at 52GB on an A100-80GB at bs32, and the practical bottleneck turned out to be the Python path, where hard eval takes 10 minutes per point.

4. Output-layer NN / reverse hybrid (HYBRID-R)

Section titled “4. Output-layer NN / reverse hybrid (HYBRID-R)”

What was tried: put the real-valued NN on the output side instead of the front. Replace pure DLGN’s readout, GroupSum, with a Linear / MLP head, and also try a sandwich with an NN at both ends. mnist20x20, 12,000 iters, seed 0, Modal A10G, DLGN k=1000 with random fixed wiring, separated learning rates (DLGN 0.03 / NN 1e-3).

Structure — the series-best sandwich

Section titled “Structure — the series-best sandwich”

The diagram below is the series-best sandwich configuration (MNIST hard 0.9731).

How to read it: the only difference from the control (pure DLGN) below is whether one orange NN has been inserted at each end. Adding ③ alone takes 0.8787 → 0.954 (+7.5pt); adding ① alone gives 0.9714; adding both gives the sandwich at 0.9731. In other words the sparse input (which ① fixes) and the readout constraint (which ③ fixes) are separate bottlenecks, and ③ can be recovered with a single minimal Linear — which is the basis for the “FPGA front end + tiny NN head” deployment (the reverse hybrid).

Section titled “6. Deep diffusion (DD-1 through DD-6) — an MECE decomposition of shape × bypass × wiring”

Bypass wiring

Section titled “Bypass wiring”

The diagram below is the expand-bypass form: solid arrows are the main path, dashed are bypasses.

How to read it: every gate has a fan-in of only 2, so “what gets mixed into the input pool” is literally how many slots for the previous layer’s features you give up. That is why raw-input bypass lost across the board in pure DLGN (§1): only once the 120 high-quality wires of the front-end NN’s interface dominate the pool does a bypass help, and then only marginally (+0.1 to +0.34pt). On the wiring side, making the dashed edges a deterministic shift-by-one (shift1) gives const+shift1 at 0.9828, on par with random wiring — deep diffusion does not need random wiring, which is the practical payoff for FPGA implementation.

10.3 Logic-DenseNet (DENSE-1) — a footing where concat is free

Section titled “10.3 Logic-DenseNet (DENSE-1) — a footing where concat is free”

What was tried: input thermo → DenseBlock (3 layers, concat) → transition (1×1 + pool) → DenseBlock (3 layers, concat) → GroupSum, with bottleneck 1×1 → LUT conv 3×3 (growth rate k). The key to the footing is that in logic gates concat is completely free (it just bundles wires and consumes no gate at all), and the three arms — dense / plain / pyramid — were compared at a matched budget (±0.16%, with an assert on exact gate-count agreement).

What it showed: the effect of dense connections is real (MNIST plain 0.7833 → dense 0.9647 = +18.1pt, CIFAR 0.3948 → 0.5671 = +17.2pt). What the failure actually consists of is the divergence “soft can learn but it does not survive going hard”, and concat provides tolerance of discretization by acting as a highway for gradients and information (the same form as the bypass findings in the deep-diffusion family). Even so, at a matched budget it does not reach the pyramid (CDDLGN) type (−0.3pt on MNIST, −2.3pt on CIFAR). Five of the 12 runs are effectively failed runs caused by OOM. The one remaining blank is dense-b2 (0.80M) — a direct comparison against pyr-b2 at a matched budget and matched bs.

11. FPGA gate budget and power (web survey)

Section titled “11. FPGA gate budget and power (web survey)”

What was tried: assuming the results above are to be put on an FPGA, survey the literature for the going rates on gate budget, packing and power (this is not measured).

QuestionConclusion
Can the CNN front-end fit on an FPGA?Yes. With hls4ml (7-bit QAT) a conv front-end of the same scale runs at 5.2µs / about 194k img/s, using 12.8% of the LUTs and 2.5% of the DSPs on a VU9P. The PCIe round trip disappears (first priority)
Can the CNN be turned into DLGN?It can, but at one million gates CIFAR-10 is ≈65% (interpolating LogicTreeNet). MNIST-class is 99.23% at 566K gates = within budget
The best move for budget efficiencyGoing to 6-LUT neurons. One million two-input gates ≈ 430k LUT6 equivalents, and FPGN-6-L reaches 76.72% on CIFAR-10 with 434k LUTs (+12pt over two-input)
Do one million gates physically fit?Spatially unrolled, an estimated 330k–500k LUTs → on a VU9P-class device (1.18M LUTs) no folding is needed. Folding is only required for edge deployment
Folding the conv stageEssentially free (the truth tables are already shared across spatial positions; even at 16M gates the unique count is 16k gates ≈ 5,500 LUTs. fold = H·W = 1024, about 195k img/s at 200MHz)
Folding the FC stageThe condition is “sharing wiring patterns across layers (going butterfly)”. Left as random wiring it breaks against a 44Mbit instruction-memory wall
The implementation cost of random wiringPost-P&R, HPWL is 3.5× and Fmax stops at 298MHz (structured wiring reaches 526MHz) = it multiplies the implementable frequency by 0.57
The trade-off formulaT·G ≤ f_clk · p · L / r (T = samples/s, G = gate count, p = packing rate [gate/LUT], L = LUT count, r = fold overhead)

As for the going rates in the literature, LogicTreeNet (two-input gates) gives 0.40M (S) 60.4% / 1.0M (interpolated) ≈64–66% / 3.08M (M) 71.0% / 16M (B) 80.2% / 61M (G) 86.3%. The mandatory techniques are residual init (−9% without it), or-pooling (−3.5% without it), and limiting kernel inputs to 2 channels + splitting into k/8 groups (to avoid routing congestion). For the first layer, thermometer encoding (R=8, 96ch) is the only option (an 8-bit first layer costs 6.2× the LUTs, 96× the DSPs and 33× the latency; with thermometer the accuracy loss is 0.0pt).

Priority order: ① co-locating the conv front-end on the FPGA (PCIe disappears, a three-order latency improvement) > ② positional folding of the conv-DLGN > ③ going 6-LUT. The limit: the packing rate p is an estimate, and every area estimate is provisional until the Verilog under export/ is synthesized with Vivado and measured.

Where we stand within the family: for CIFAR, accuracy against gate count has LogicTreeNet’s log-linear line as its baseline (+11.9pt per 10× gates), and both difflogic and the learned-wiring family sit about +2.9pt off it in extrapolation — nobody has broken the line by an order of magnitude yet. The only thing confirmed to move the line is going 6-LUT. Conversely, binary/quantized CNNs use 29–56× more gate-equivalents for the same accuracy, so on gate efficiency alone the logic-network side is clearly ahead. Speed splits into two clusters, the ns class (fully unrolled, II=1) and the µs class (folding / quantized CNNs / GPU), with the 10²–10⁴ ns range in between essentially empty. And our hybrids and checkerboards are on a different footing — only the back-end DLGN part goes on that horizontal axis, and as long as a real-valued NN front-end remains, they cannot in principle join the ns-class cluster. A head-on comparison with the pure-gate camp only becomes possible once “DLGN-izing the conv stage + going 6-LUT” puts us on the baseline.
