Auditing the "world first" claims

This project holds several claims of the form “the first in the world to do X with logic gates.” This page is the record of what happened when we looked for counterexamples rather than defending them. Verdicts, nearest prior work, the exact qualified wording, and the claim we retracted are all published as they are.

The point is to make the strength of the claims comparable side by side, so the basis of each verdict (search queries, survey date, the load-bearing qualifier) is stated per claim, and claims resting on thin evidence are laid out in a way that makes their thinness visible.

1. Method of the audit

Section titled “1. Method of the audit”

1.1 Falsification search (looking for counterexamples, not confirmations)

Section titled “1.1 Falsification search (looking for counterexamples, not confirmations)”

If you collect support for a claim, confirmation bias quietly turns “we did not find it” into “it does not exist.” So for each claim the searches are run in the posture of hunting for the paper that kills it.

  • 4–10 queries per claim (general web search + arXiv API full-text/abstract search + close reading of individual abs pages)
  • The absence of a hit is itself recorded as a negative finding (e.g. the arXiv API query abs:"logic gate" AND abs:"text classification" returned 0 total hits, and abs:"logic gate network" AND abs:"distillation" also returned 0 total hits)
  • The Related Work sections of adjacent papers are followed to check, second-hand, for hidden direct competitors
  • The full list of queries used and the survey date are kept per claim (so a third party can re-run the same searches later)

1.2 Three-valued verdicts

Section titled “1.2 Three-valued verdicts”
VerdictMeaning
Survives (no counterexample)No counterexample was found within the scope of the exhaustive search
Conditionally survives (close prior work)Survives as long as the qualifiers are kept. Drop even one qualifier and it is refuted
Refuted (counterexample exists)A counterexample exists. The claim is retracted or narrowed

For “conditionally survives” we always write down which qualifier is load-bearing. For instance, the claim about autoregressive generation on FPGA rests on the triple qualifier “real FPGA hardware × text generation × non-instruction-set”, and dropping any one of the three puts it in the line of fire of existing work.

1.3 Rechecking freshness

Section titled “1.3 Rechecking freshness”

This field moves fast, and in 2025–2026 one gap after another was filled. So past verdicts are not treated as fixed: the policy is to re-audit periodically and downgrade. In the most recent recheck (2026-09-05) no claim was refuted over the two-month interval, but one was downgraded from ✅ to 🟡, and one was judged to need its comparison text revised.

1.4 Limits of this audit (stated up front)

Section titled “1.4 Limits of this audit (stated up front)”
  • Preprints from the last few weeks are out of scope. Work under review or unpublished parallel work cannot be caught
  • Non-English literature is out of scope (Japanese only partially covered; Chinese, Portuguese and others not surveyed)
  • Full-text search of non-arXiv and paywalled literature is only indirect. FPGA-side workshops (FPL / FCCM / ASAP / ICFPT) and embedded-systems journals in particular have a low arXiv rate and may be buried
  • No systematic patent search has been done. Patents on RAM-based NN classifiers do exist, so anyone considering a filing needs a separate professional search
  • Some verdicts stop at the abstract. Close reading of the full text may force the qualified form to be narrowed further

2. Definition of the strength badges

Section titled “2. Definition of the strength badges”

The “strength of the evidence” for a claim is made explicit as an axis separate from the verdict itself.

BadgeMeaning
S1Paper-grade absence evidence (search queries, survey date, and the scope limits of the claim all written out)
S2Audited by falsification search (nearest prior work stated; qualified form mandatory)
🟡Close prior work exists (survives only in qualified form)
S3Claim only, not audited
RetractedRetracted after a counterexample was found
—Reproduction or port (no world-first claim)

S3 and — do not appear in these tables. S3 is the badge for claims that have not been through the audit, and the policy is to send them to the audit before they are listed on this page. — goes to work that never claimed a world first in the first place, such as DLGN reproductions or ports of existing methods.

4. Record of a retraction — A4, “the logic reservoir”

Section titled “4. Record of a retraction — A4, “the logic reservoir””

Keeping this section is what makes the other verdicts worth believing. If we went around erasing retracted claims, the surviving claims could be assumed to get the same treatment.

  • The original claim: a configuration that uses a frozen random logic circuit as a reservoir and trains only the readout layer is a world first
  • Verdict: Refuted (counterexample exists). Found in the re-survey of 2026-07-10
  • Counterexamples:
    • LUTNet-RC (IEEE ICFPT 2023) — LUT-based reservoir computing
    • Random Boolean Network (RBN) reservoirs — classical work since the 2000s (Snyder et al. ~2013 and others, two decades of accumulation)
    • Reservoirs built from CMOS gates (ACM 2021)
  • Why we missed it: we were searching in our own vocabulary of “differentiable logic gate networks”, so we never reached the vocabulary of the RBN / reservoir computing side (a different lineage in an adjacent field). This failure is what prompted us to widen falsification search into “search in the vocabulary of adjacent fields too”
  • The corrected claim: the core of “frozen random logic circuit + learned readout” belongs to prior work. Our contribution is limited to integration into a differentiable framework, HDL output from the same IR, and sharing learning rules with the other components (GPT/LSTM). Any write-up must cite LUTNet-RC and RBN reservoirs
  • Fallout: the “world first” phrasing left in model docstrings, the roadmap and reports was retracted and rewritten. Overstatements of the same kind (WaveNet, detection, text classification) have since been corrected to qualified forms one by one in later audits

5. Four-quadrant map

Section titled “5. Four-quadrant map”

Horizontal axis = strength of novelty (left: survives only in qualified form → right: strong absence evidence), vertical axis = how far the measurements have got (bottom: calibration pending / no full run → top: measured and solid).

The meaning of each quadrant and what sits in it, in text as well.

Measured and solid (top)Calibration pending / no full run (bottom)
Strong novelty (right)Flagship. LogicGPT (A1/A2), fully logical diffusion (B1), LogicVAE (B2), LogicLSTM (A3), SST-2 KD (W3). The first candidates for a paper come from hereWhitespace but unproven. LogicMixer (B4a), LogicGAN (B3), LogicResNet (B4b). The area where we should stop talking about novelty and produce numbers first
Survives only in qualified form (left)Compete on results. TextCNN (W1), 8 emotions (W2), CenterNet-LUT (B4d/W5), Attention Seq2Seq (W4), framework breadth (B5). Lead with the numbers reached and the differences, not with “first”Needs redefinition. LogicUNet (B4c), LogicWaveNet (B4e), LogicReservoir (A4, retracted). Either narrow the qualified form or drop the claim

There are two points to take from this.

  • A thin top-right is the honest picture of where we are. The stronger the novelty claim, the shallower the measurements tend to be; conversely, text classification and detection, where the measurements are solid, have close prior work
  • The bottom-left is the zone for deciding to drop a claim. The retracted A4 is still there because the implementation and the measurements themselves (frozenness, fading memory, convergence from training the readout alone) remain valuable after the retraction — only the novelty claim was withdrawn

6. “World first” is not a proof of absolute absence

Section titled “6. “World first” is not a proof of absolute absence”

Every verdict on this page is limited to the following meaning.

As of the survey date, in the qualified form as written, an exhaustive search with the queries as written failed to find a counterexample.

This is not a proof of non-existence. As stated in §1.4 above, preprints from the last few weeks, non-English literature, paywalled literature, patents and unpublished implementations are all out of scope.

Accordingly, when the novelty of this project is mentioned in external materials, papers or talks, the operational rule is to observe these three points.

  1. Write it in the qualified form. Use the “qualified wording” from the verdict tables verbatim and do not drop the qualifiers (several claims are refuted the moment a single qualifier is removed)
  2. Always state the nearest prior work alongside it. Never write “first” without showing the difference
  3. Attach the survey date. Verdicts have a shelf life. The field moves fast, and a two-month recheck already downgraded one claim

When a new claim is raised, look for the counterexample first. If one turns up, write it on this page instead of hiding it.
