"DLGN" covers three lineages whose properties differ considerably. This page sorts out
**what actually qualifies as a DLGN**, the families inside it, and the break between all of them
and the "compile an ordinary MLP to an FPGA" approach. The prerequisite is
[What is a DLGN](/en/explanation/dlgn/).

## 1. What qualifies as a DLGN

At the core: **an NN whose neuron is a logic gate / LUT (a Boolean function) rather than a
multiply-accumulate (MAC) unit**. During training, "which gate each neuron becomes" is relaxed into
something differentiable and learned by gradients; after training, argmax fixes each gate and the
model becomes a **multiplication-free, purely Boolean circuit**.

Three axes decide it.

1. Is the **unit of computation** a Boolean function (gate / LUT), or a MAC?
2. Is **gate selection learned** (softmax relaxation or EFD → discretization)?
3. Is **inference multiplication-free logic**?

Satisfy all three and it's a DLGN. Within that, the families below differ in
**fan-in, parameterization, wiring, and how the gates are obtained**.

## 2. The three families

| | **Arithmetic DLGN**<br/>(LogicNets family) | **Native DLGN**<br/>(difflogic family) | **6-LUT DLGN**<br/>(DWN family) |
|---|---|---|---|
| How gates are obtained | **exact lowering** from quantization + sparsification + popcount thresholds (a mathematical derivation, not learning) | **16 gates under a softmax, learned freely by gradients** | n-input LUTs **learned with EFD gradients** |
| Semantics | **arithmetic is preserved** (popcount tree = addition, threshold = activation) | any free composition of Boolean functions (no arithmetic needed) | any n-input Boolean function |
| Fan-in | N after sparsification (e.g. 32/48) → adder tree | **2 inputs (16 gates)** | **6 inputs (LUT6)** |
| Wiring | fixed correspondence to the important inputs picked by sparsification (it means something) | fixed random (the wiring carries no meaning) | **learnable mapping** |
| What the "weights" really are | **weights = circuit topology** (the weights are the circuit) | a choice of "which gate" (thrown away after training) | LUT contents + wiring |
| Scale on MNIST | large: about 418k LUTs | small: under 24k gates (about 1/50) | tiny: about 4.6k LUTs (FPGA) |
| In this library | **the output of a conversion**, emitted by `export/lowering.py` and friends | `layers/lut.py` (softmax) + the various models | `models/dwn.py` |

Sources: Umuroglu et al. (LogicNets, 2020) / Petersen et al. (difflogic, NeurIPS 2022) /
Bacellar et al. (DWN, ICML 2024).

## 3. How this differs from "ordinary MLP → FPGA"

Even among circuits that run on an FPGA, there are degrees: **is multiplication kept or removed**,
and **is the arithmetic meaning kept or discarded**.

| Approach | Computation | Multiplication | FPGA resources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MLP → FPGA (the ordinary way)** | MAC (sum of weight × activation) | **stays** | DSPs / multipliers + adders |
| **Quantized MLP → LUT** (= arithmetic DLGN) | the MAC becomes a **popcount adder tree** (quantize + sparsify + enumerate truth tables) | **gone**, but **the arithmetic meaning is preserved** | LUTs (adder tree) |
| **Native DLGN** (difflogic) | each neuron is a **learned two-input logic gate** | never there to begin with | LUTs (logic) |
| **6-LUT DLGN** (DWN) | learned six-input LUTs + learned wiring | never there to begin with | direct LUT6 mapping |

The break is at the third step. **"Ordinary MLP → FPGA" merely turns multiplication into circuitry;
inside, it's still an MLP.** **Arithmetic DLGN converts a quantized MLP's arithmetic exactly into
LUTs** (weights = circuit; multiplication disappears but the additive structure survives). And
**native DLGN throws the arithmetic away entirely and learns logic from the start** — that is the
genuinely discontinuous leap.

## 4. Why converting an existing model doesn't work

"Repurpose a strong pretrained MLP as a native DLGN and it should come out stronger" is a natural
thought, but we measured three routes in-house (distillation / transferred initialization from the
sparsified fan-in sets / layerwise binary distillation) and **none of them beat training from
scratch**. Transferred initialization was much worse.

The taxonomy explains why. **Arithmetic DLGN (dense arithmetic, weights = circuit) and native DLGN
(sparse, free Boolean logic) are as unalike as it gets**, and a native DLGN's strength is exactly
that it can learn gates as free discrete logic. Transcribing arithmetic structure onto it
**confines that freedom to the mold of arithmetic**, which acts as a constraint rather than a head
start.

→ **Conversion is worth it only when a hardware-faithful arithmetic circuit is the goal. If a
native DLGN is the goal, training from scratch is the answer.** Knowledge distillation from a
strong teacher only means something when the teacher is substantially stronger than a
from-scratch DLGN.

## 5. Where this library's models sit

**Nearly every trainable model here is a native DLGN** (`n_inputs=2`,
`parameterization='softmax'`); what differs is **the architecture**, not the gate scheme.

| Model | Family | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **dlgn** (`layers/lut.py` + `LutStack`) | native DLGN | 2-input, 16-gate softmax, stacked MLP-style |
| **conv-dlgn** (`conv.py::LutConv2d`) | native DLGN (conv) | tree structure + OR pooling (LogicTreeNet family) |
| **rddlgn** | native DLGN (recurrent seq2seq) | LutStack + a recurrent encoder-decoder |
| **attention** | native DLGN (attention seq2seq) | **attention and all, entirely logic** (LogicAttention) |
| **logic-gpt** | native DLGN (decoder-only) | LutStack + causal self-attention |
| **DWN** (`models/dwn.py`) | **6-LUT DLGN** | 6-input LUTs + learned wiring (the only non-2-input model) |
| **Arithmetic DLGN** | — (not a model but **the output of a conversion**) | popcount adder-tree circuit |

## 6. A caution on "why not just use 6-LUTs everywhere for FPGA?"

For inference and FPGA area, 6-LUTs do win (the physical cell is a LUT6, so six inputs map onto one
cell while two inputs waste most of it; DWN solves MNIST in 4.6K LUTs, about 1/18 of FINN's 83K).
But **the weaknesses of 6-LUT DLGN are all on the training side**.

1. **EFD gradients are approximate** — 2-input softmax gives exact gradients, whereas an n-input LUT
   is an approximation, and optimization grows less stable as fan-in rises
2. **It overfits easily** — a capacity of $2^{2^6}$ is plenty for memorization, so regularization
   such as Spectral Regularization is all but mandatory
3. **Learnable mapping is fragile** — in our measurements the learned-wiring version has lost to
   fixed wiring, and hard accuracy has collapsed outright; the DWN paper itself treats
   "learned wiring in the first layer only" as the standard practice
4. **Diminishing returns on fan-in** (it plateaus around six inputs)
5. **Training memory blows up** (64 entries per gate plus the wiring matrix)
6. **It's slow on CPU/GPU** (it becomes a table lookup, which destroys bit parallelism)

difflogic chose two inputs not because it is optimal for FPGAs, but for **clean training (16 gates
with exact gradients) and CPU/GPU speed**. The right conclusion is to treat
**fan-in as a knob set by the deployment target**.

| Goal | Favored fan-in |
|---|---|
| FPGA area | 6 |
| CPU / GPU inference speed | 2 |
| Training stability | 2 |

## 7. Where hybrids (partial logic-gate conversion) sit

There is also a school that replaces **only part** of the network with LUTs. The representative
example is LL-ViT (Nag et al., ICFPT 2025), which **replaces only the MLP (channel mixer) of a ViT
with LUT neurons and leaves attention as an ordinary Transformer**.

- Overall: a **hybrid (partial LGN)** — not everything is a gate
- MLP: n-input LUTs trained with DWN's EFD (= the 6-LUT / DWN family)
- Attention: ordinary multi-head self-attention (**multiplication present = not a DLGN**)
- Input/output bridges: thermometer encoding (reals → bits) and conditional summation
  (4-bit quantization = arithmetic remains)
- In this library: `hybrid/` (`replace_mlp_with_lut`) and `encoding/bridges.py`

By contrast, this library's DLGN attention models turn **attention itself entirely into logic**.
Same "LUT × attention" label, different position. In terms of the three families, the accurate
two-level reading is that **the LUTs inside LL-ViT are 6-LUT (DWN) family, while the model as a
whole is not a pure DLGN but a hybrid of LUTs and a Transformer**.
