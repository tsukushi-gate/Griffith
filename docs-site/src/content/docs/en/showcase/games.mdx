In games and classic control tasks, the strengths of logic circuits and NNs (classic NEAT with real-valued weights) split clearly.
**Logic circuits are strong when the task is a "go or no-go" decision; NNs are strong when thrust must be tuned finely.**

## Flappy Bird: an 11-gate landslide

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="flappy_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic circuit (11 gates) playing Flappy Bird."
	meta="Solved in both runs (fitness 48.64 / 54.63)"
/>

The observation is just 4 values: the next pipe's position, the height difference to the gap's center, the bird's height, and its vertical speed. The circuit receives them as 32 bits.
**The logic circuit solved it in both runs with 11 gates.** The NN stayed at a fitness of about 11 even after 900 generations, 3.5 times the logic circuit's budget.
Deciding whether you fit through the gap is a discrete judgment, exactly where logic gates shine.

Removing gates without changing a single step of the solving behavior shrank the 11 gates down to 4 and 2.

## 2D race car: only the logic circuit completes a lap

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="car2d_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic circuit (23 gates) lapping the track."
/>

Seven distance sensors and the speed are converted into 64 bits, and the circuit picks one of "forward, left, right, brake".
**The logic circuit completed a lap in both runs with 22-23 gates.** The NN (66-91 parameters) did not complete a lap in either run.

## Snake

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="snake_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic circuit (14 gates) playing Snake."
/>

The observation is already 11 bits of 0/1, so it goes straight into the circuit.
The logic circuit reached the 10-food target in both runs with 12-14 gates; the NN did so in 1 of 2.
The single best food collector, however, was the NN's lucky run (23.37 versus the logic circuit's best of 17.14), so on strength the circuit loses.

## CartPole and LunarLander

### CartPole: both solve it right away

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="cartpole_dlgn.mp4"

/>

The classic task of keeping a pole on a cart from falling. The logic circuit with 8 gates and the NN with 4 parameters both solved it right away.

### LunarLander: the NN wins

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="lunarlander_nn.mp4"
	caption="NN (12-33 parameters) landing."
/>

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="lunarlander_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic circuit (19-20 gates) attempting to land."
/>

Land a lunar module by firing thrusters to control attitude and speed.
**The NN landed successfully in both runs with 12-33 parameters.** The logic circuit grew to 19-20 gates
but failed verification under different initial conditions. Fine-grained thrust control suits real-valued weights better.

## Robot arms

### Carrying an object with a two-joint arm

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="arm2d_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic-circuit (29 gates) arm: approach, grasp, carry, release, in order."
/>

The task requires going through stages in order: approach the object, grasp it, carry it to the target, and release it.
Neither method reached the target score, but the 29-gate circuit carried the object all the way in 33 of 2000 attempts (1.65%).
Random actions manage 0 of 2000, so it is not luck, but all we can say is that it "sometimes manages to carry".

### Reacher (move the fingertip to a target)

<ShowcaseVideo
	name="reacher_dlgn.mp4"
	caption="Logic-circuit (36 gates) Reacher."
/>

MuJoCo's Reacher. Each of the two joints is driven with one of "positive, zero, negative".
The logic circuit (36 gates) and the NN (32 parameters) both improved on the first generation, but the gains were small: a partial success.
